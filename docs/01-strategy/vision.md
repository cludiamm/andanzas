# Andanzas — Vision

*Stage 1 artifact. Version 1.0 — August 2026.*
*Built on the three gaps in `methodology.md` §7. The archived v0 strategy brief in `docs/00-case-study-v0/` does not constrain it.*

---

## The vision

> ### Trust the plan. Know the gaps.
>
> Andanzas is for the person who plans — the one carrying everyone's trip on their own time. It does the research and shows its work: where each fact came from, when it was last true, and the short list of things still worth a phone call. You keep the trip that's yours. You just stop paying for it in doubt.

---

## Who it is for

**The planner.** Not the group.

Every trip requires exactly one motivated planner; no planner, no trip (§1). Value is delivered to that person, and growth comes from passengers who become planners for their own next trip.

What the planner actually absorbs is not labour — it is **risk**. When the museum is shut on arrival, nobody turns to the passenger. That accountability, carried unpaid and unrecognised, is what makes the job heavy. It is also the best explanation of the one live commercial signal in the methodology: on the Patagonia trip a friend pays the planner, and what he is paying for is not hours. He is paying because someone else is carrying it if the booking is wrong.

## Why it exists — calibrated trust

Travel information is abundant and almost none of it is verifiable.

The nine evenings a planner spends are not spent *finding* things. Information is free and everywhere. They are spent **authenticating** it: which blog is current, which review is astroturfed, which opening hours the venue forgot to update after the renovation. The planner has been paying in hours for confidence, not for facts.

Andanzas exists so a planner can know not only what the plan says, but **how much of it can be trusted — and exactly which parts still can't.**

This is deliberately a claim about *epistemics*, not about time saved. Saving time is a benefit, not a purpose; every tool in a crowded and largely free category claims it. Calibration is the thing the category does not have.

**Why now.** Unverifiable travel content is being produced at zero marginal cost by systems that never went. As it floods the channels planners used to research trips, verified information becomes scarcer and more valuable each year. This is a scarcity play built into an abundance trend — and it should be tested rather than assumed.

## What it does not promise

The product does **not** promise that everything is handled and nothing needs checking.

That promise removes the last human safety check, and every stale fact then converts directly into a wasted afternoon in another country — delayed, asymmetric, unfixable on the ground. It is the failure shape ADR 0001 was written to prevent.

The promise is the co-admin's, not the travel agent's: *"I've confirmed these six, you should phone about the seventh."*

Three consequences follow, and they are binding:

1. **All of the value lives in the ratio.** 80% confirmed / 20% flagged is a strong product. 30/70 is a liability wearing honesty as a costume. Calibration is harder than accuracy — being right 90% of the time is straightforward; knowing *which* 90% is the problem.
2. **A flag must be actionable, or it is homework.** *"Verify opening hours"* is a task handed back to the user. *"Their site contradicts Google — call and ask about Monday"* is a co-admin.
3. **Coverage degrades gracefully.** Freshness is disclosed, not guaranteed, so a weaker corpus means more flags rather than no launch. The expansion gate is therefore **unflagged error rate** — a flagged miss was never a broken promise.

## Horizon

A vision horizon two years out cannot arbitrate a priority fight next month. This one is a ladder, at rising stakes:

| When | Who | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | Claudia's sister, planning unassisted with a generic AI | that the problem is real, and where trust actually breaks |
| **Nov 2026** | Patagonia — Claudia planning | that the methodology encodes correctly |
| **2028** | China — Claudia and friends, real money | that a planner delivers a trip they could not have delivered manually |

Claudia is the right tester for **correctness** and the wrong tester for **trust** — she cannot experience trust being transferred, because she knows how it was made.

## What this vision rules out

- **Auto-booking and auto-cutting.** The system proposes, the planner disposes (§8.1).
- **Feature-parity chasing** against free itinerary apps. Budgets, maps, calendars and collaborative itineraries are table stakes; they belong in the product and must not lead the roadmap or the pitch.
- **Any surface that shows a fact without showing where it came from and when it was last checked.** Expensive and permanent.
- **Becoming a group-social product.** Passengers are how the planner's work is judged, not the customer.
- **Claiming what a local knows.** A local knows things no corpus holds. The achievable version is what a friend who just went would tell you.

## The assumption under test

Pre-registered before evidence was read:

> **A planner who did not do the research can act with confidence on it — provided they can see what was verified, against what, and when.**

Claudia had no direct experience of being handed research and trusting it, so this is currently an intuition carrying the whole vision. Evidence available:

- Her sister's Claude planning sessions for Japan / Korea / China — a real non-expert planner, unassisted, on a real trip.
- Claudia's own Patagonia sessions — not evidence of trust transfer, but her corrections to the model are the methodology expressed as diffs.
- Three questions to her sister, best asked before departure: what did she verify herself and why those things; what did she accept unchecked and why she was comfortable; has anything turned out to be wrong.

If the evidence contradicts the assumption, this document changes.
